The ingredients are the raw material a forecast learns from. Two so far, both measured by us from real ocean data. In plain words:

El Niño / La Niña
the natural warming (El Niño) or cooling (La Niña) of the tropical Pacific that tilts weather around the world.
Anomaly
how far from normal, in °C. “+1” means one degree warmer than the 1991 to 2020 average for that time of year.
Niño 3.4
the patch of the Pacific whose temperature is the headline El Niño signal. Our first ingredient.
Warm water volume
how much warm water is stored just below the surface. It builds up months before an El Niño, so it is an early warning. Our second ingredient.

Before building any forecast, we set the rules of the game: what a fair test looks like, and the score to beat. Here is our approach next to how the professionals work.

How the experts do it

Weather centres like NOAA run dozens of physics models on supercomputers, add AI, and blend them into one official forecast (the “plume”). Very accurate, and impossible to reproduce alone.

What we do here

One simple model we build and fully understand, from our own data, judged honestly against dumb guesses. The aim is to grasp how it works and get close to the official forecast, not to out-predict NOAA.

Why start simple: a model you cannot explain or test honestly is worthless. So we prove the data and the scoring first, then add a model only where it genuinely beats the dumb guesses. Our first model does exactly that, most of all at long range, where the warm water volume carries the signal (scoreboard below).

How we score a forecast, without cheating

The scoreboard: the dumb guesses vs our model

Lead
how far ahead we predict: 1, 3 or 6 months.
Baseline
a dumb guess a real model must beat to prove it learned anything.
Persistence
the guess “the next months look like this one.”
Climatology
the guess “it will be normal” (zero anomaly).
Model
our own simple model, reading today’s temperature, the warm water volume, and the season.
RMSE
the average size of the miss, in °C. Lower is better.
ACC
whether the ups and downs line up over time: 0 is no skill, 1 is perfect.
